Key Definitions in ODL

The shared vocabulary you need before classifying, planning, or designing any ODL course

A Shared Vocabulary

Before you can classify a programme, calculate Student Learning Time, or design a course, you need to be fluent in the terminology that governs ODL delivery. These definitions are drawn from the University's guideline and aligned with MQA COPPA requirements. They are not optional — they are the basis for every planning and compliance decision you will make.

Let's start with the two most important thresholds in ODL.

> 60%
ODL Programme Threshold

More than 60% of graduating credits must come from ODL courses

≥ 80%
ODL Course Threshold

At least 80% of Student Learning Time must be delivered via ODL mode

These two thresholds work together. A programme is classified as ODL based on the proportion of graduating credits contributed by ODL courses, and a course is classified as ODL based on the proportion of its SLT delivered through online and distance learning approaches.

Remember: The programme calculation is based on graduating credits, not the number of courses. This distinction matters when programmes combine ODL and conventional courses.

Delivery Methods

ODL courses use a combination of four delivery approaches. Each has distinct characteristics and plays a different role in your course design.

Delivery Method
Synchronous Online Learning

Learning activities conducted in real time through digital platforms, where students and academic staff interact simultaneously. Examples include live online lectures, webinars, virtual tutorials, and real-time consultations. Counts as contact hours and ODL delivery.

Delivery Method
Asynchronous Online Learning

Structured learning activities completed at the student's own pace and time, without requiring simultaneous participation. Examples include recorded lectures, discussion forums, digital modules, and guided exercises. Counts as ODL delivery but not contact hours.

Delivery Method
Independent Learning

Self-directed learning activities undertaken by students outside scheduled sessions. Examples include reading, assignment preparation, research, and portfolio development. Counts as SLT. May count towards ODL delivery when integrated into the learning design.

Delivery Method
Physical Face-to-Face Learning

Activities requiring physical attendance at a designated location. Examples include labs, clinical training, studio sessions, and fieldwork. Counts as contact hours and SLT but does not contribute to the 80% ODL requirement. Generally limited to a maximum of 20% of total SLT.

Critical distinction: Synchronous and asynchronous online learning both count as ODL delivery. Independent learning may count when integrated into the ODL design. Physical face-to-face learning counts as SLT but never counts towards the 80% ODL threshold.

Supporting Definitions

Beyond the delivery methods, these terms appear repeatedly throughout the guideline and in every planning document you will prepare.

Time & Credit
Student Learning Time (SLT)

The total number of hours required for a student to achieve the learning outcomes of a course. Includes synchronous learning, asynchronous learning, independent learning, assessments, and any face-to-face sessions. 1 credit = 40 SLT hours (per MQF).

Learning Design
Self-Instructional Material (SIM)

Structured learning materials specifically designed to support independent learning in ODL. Includes learning outcomes, content, activities, self-assessment, and references. Not the same as lecture notes or slides.

Interaction
Regular and Substantive Interaction (RSI)

Meaningful and ongoing engagement between academic staff and students. "Regular" means consistent and systematic. "Substantive" means it contributes directly to learning — not just administrative announcements.

Documentation
Course Information (Table 4)

The official MQA-prescribed document that specifies CLOs, learning activities, assessment methods, SLT distribution, and alignment with programme outcomes. For ODL courses, it must show the ODL delivery breakdown.

Infrastructure
Learning Management System (LMS)

The University's approved digital platform for delivering content, administering assessments, facilitating communication, tracking engagement, and managing grades for ODL courses.

Quality
Continuous Quality Improvement (CQI)

A systematic and ongoing process of monitoring, evaluating, reviewing, and improving ODL programmes based on evidence, feedback, performance indicators, and learning outcomes achievement.

Putting Definitions into Practice

So far, you have seen each definition in isolation — the thresholds, the four delivery methods, and the supporting terms that appear across every ODL planning document. But in practice, these terms do not sit in neat categories. They interact with one another inside real course designs, and that is where mistakes tend to happen. Misclassifying a single delivery method can distort your SLT calculation, which in turn affects whether your course meets the 80% threshold, which then affects whether your programme meets the 60% requirement.

The case below shows how easily this can go wrong. As you read it, try to spot the classification errors before reading the explanation.

Case: The Terminology Trap

Assoc. Prof. Lim is preparing Table 4 for his ODL course. He allocates 10 hours of recorded video lectures and labels them as "synchronous online learning" because the videos were recorded by the lecturer. He also allocates 5 hours of "independent learning" for students to browse YouTube for relevant content, without providing any guidance or structure. The total SLT for his 3-credit course is 120 hours, of which he claims 96 hours (80%) as ODL delivery.

What's wrong here? Assoc. Prof. Lim has made two classification errors. First, recorded video lectures are asynchronous online learning (not synchronous), because students access them at their own time without real-time interaction. Second, unstructured YouTube browsing does not qualify as independent learning for ODL purposes — independent learning must be guided, structured, and aligned with Course Learning Outcomes. These misclassifications mean his ODL delivery percentage is incorrectly calculated, and the course may not actually meet the 80% threshold.
