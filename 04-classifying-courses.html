Classifying ODL Courses — The 80% Rule

How to determine whether an individual course qualifies as an ODL course

On the previous page, you classified a programme using the 60% rule. But that programme-level calculation depends on a prior step: determining whether each individual course qualifies as an ODL course. This page zooms in to the course level and shows you exactly how that determination is made.

The Rule

A course is considered an ODL course when at least 80% of the total Student Learning Time (SLT) is delivered through ODL mode. ODL mode includes synchronous online learning, asynchronous online learning, and independent learning that is intentionally integrated into the ODL learning design.

Physical face-to-face learning — activities conducted in classrooms, labs, studios, or field sites — does not count towards the 80%. In fact, physical face-to-face activities should generally not exceed 20% of the total SLT of an ODL course.

The logic is simple: If at least 80% of the learning happens online or at a distance, the course is an ODL course. If more than 20% requires physical attendance, the course does not qualify.

What Counts Towards the 80%?

Not all learning activities are treated equally. Here is how each delivery method relates to the ODL classification:

Delivery Method Counts as ODL Delivery? Counts as Contact Hours?
Synchronous Online Learning — live online sessions, webinars, virtual tutorials Yes Yes
Asynchronous Online Learning — recorded lectures, discussion forums, digital modules Yes No
Independent Learning — guided reading, assignment preparation, research (when integrated into ODL design) Yes No
Physical Face-to-Face — labs, studios, clinical training, fieldwork No Yes
Watch the independent learning caveat: Independent learning only counts towards the 80% when it is intentionally designed and integrated into the ODL learning environment. Unstructured or unguided activities — such as telling students to "find resources online" without guidance — do not qualify.

Qualifies as ODL Course

Example: Research Methods in Education (3 credits = 120 hours SLT)

Learning Activity Delivery Method Hours
Live online seminars (weekly, 2 hrs × 14 weeks) Synchronous online 28
Recorded lectures and video modules Asynchronous online 20
Guided readings and annotated bibliography Independent learning (structured) 30
Online discussion forum participation Asynchronous online 12
Assignment preparation and submission Independent learning (guided) 20
On-campus research ethics workshop Physical face-to-face 10
ODL delivery total 110 of 120 (91.7%)

Result: 110 out of 120 hours (91.7%) are delivered through ODL mode. The 10-hour on-campus workshop (8.3%) is well within the 20% limit. This course qualifies as an ODL course.

Does NOT Qualify as ODL Course

Example: Clinical Assessment (4 credits = 160 hours SLT)

Learning Activity Delivery Method Hours
Live online case discussions Synchronous online 20
Recorded demonstration videos Asynchronous online 15
Clinical reading and case analysis Independent learning (structured) 25
Hospital-based clinical rotations Physical face-to-face 60
Simulation lab practice Physical face-to-face 20
Online assessment and e-portfolio Asynchronous online 20
ODL delivery total 80 of 160 (50%)

Result: Only 50% of the SLT is delivered through ODL mode. The 80 hours of physical face-to-face activities (hospital rotations + simulation lab) push the course well below the 80% threshold. This course does not qualify as an ODL course — and that is perfectly acceptable. Not every course needs to be ODL.

Important Considerations

  • The classification is based on the proportion of Student Learning Time delivered through ODL approaches, not the number or type of online activities offered.
  • All ODL SLT claimed should be supported by appropriate learning design, Self-Instructional Materials (SIMs), digital learning resources, online activities, assessments, and evidence of student engagement.
  • Physical face-to-face activities should only be included where there is a clear pedagogical, practical, professional, or regulatory justification.
  • If your course falls just below the 80% threshold, consider whether any face-to-face activities could be redesigned for online delivery without compromising learning outcomes.

Applying the 80% Rule to Your Course

You now have the tools to classify individual courses. Combined with the 60% programme-level rule from the previous page, you can determine whether an entire programme qualifies as ODL. The process is always the same: classify each course first, then calculate the programme-level percentage based on graduating credits.

Case: The Borderline Programme

Prof. Kamala is developing a Master of Public Health (40 graduating credits, 8 courses). She has classified her courses as follows:

Course Credits % ODL SLT ODL Course?
Epidemiology490%Yes
Biostatistics485%Yes
Health Policy482%Yes
Qualitative Methods480%Yes
Community Practicum A845%No
Community Practicum B840%No
Research Design488%Yes
Dissertation492%Yes

Does this programme qualify as ODL? Six of eight courses qualify as ODL courses, contributing 24 credits out of 40 graduating credits (60%). This is exactly 60% — not more than 60%. The programme does not qualify. The threshold requires more than 60%, so Prof. Kamala would need at least one of the non-ODL courses to be redesigned to meet the 80% ODL threshold, or she would need to adjust the credit structure.

This case illustrates a critical nuance: the 60% threshold is a strict greater-than condition. Meeting it exactly is not enough.

He plans 24 hours of live online seminars, 18 hours of recorded lectures, 30 hours of guided independent reading, and 18 hours of online forum discussions. The remaining hours are allocated to two on-campus lab sessions. Does this course qualify as an ODL course?

  • B. Yes — the ODL delivery is 90 hours (75%), but the lab sessions do not count because they are optional
  • C. No — the ODL delivery is 90 hours (75%), which falls below the 80% threshold. The course does not qualify.
  • D. No — the ODL delivery is 120 hours (100%), but lab sessions cannot be included in an ODL course at all

Question 2: The Independent Learning Trap

Dr. Lee is preparing Table 4 for her 3-credit ODL course (120 hours SLT). Her SLT breakdown is: 20 hours synchronous online, 25 hours asynchronous online, 50 hours independent learning (students told to "search for and read relevant articles online"), and 25 hours physical face-to-face lab work. She claims 95 hours (79.2%) as ODL delivery. What is the most significant problem with her classification?

  • A. The synchronous online hours are too low to count as ODL delivery
  • B. The 50 hours of independent learning are unstructured and unguided — they should not be counted towards ODL delivery, which reduces her actual ODL percentage significantly
  • C. Physical face-to-face lab work cannot be included in any ODL course
  • D. Her ODL percentage of 79.2% is actually above the 80% threshold once rounding is applied

Question 3: Programme-Level Impact

A Master's programme has 40 graduating credits across 5 courses. Course A (8 credits, 85% ODL SLT), Course B (8 credits, 90% ODL SLT), Course C (8 credits, 78% ODL SLT), Course D (8 credits, 82% ODL SLT), and Course E (8 credits, 88% ODL SLT). How many credits count towards the ODL programme classification, and does the programme qualify?

  • A. 40 credits count (all courses are above 75% ODL) — the programme qualifies
  • B. 32 credits count (Courses A, B, D, and E meet the 80% threshold; Course C at 78% does not) — 32 of 40 = 80%, the programme qualifies
  • C. 24 credits count (only 3 courses meet the threshold) — 24 of 40 = 60%, the programme does not qualify
  • D. 40 credits count (the average ODL percentage across all courses exceeds 80%) — the programme qualifies

Summary

A course qualifies as an ODL course when at least 80% of its Student Learning Time is delivered through online and distance learning approaches. Synchronous online, asynchronous online, and structured independent learning all count. Physical face-to-face activities do not count and should generally not exceed 20% of total SLT.
