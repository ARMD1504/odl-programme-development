Module 3 • Planning and Designing Your ODL Course

Student Learning Time (SLT) Framework

Understanding how SLT drives credit allocation, course classification, and ODL compliance

In the previous module, you learned about the four delivery methods available in ODL and how each counts — or does not count — towards the ODL delivery requirement. Now it is time to put those methods together into a complete course design. The starting point is the Student Learning Time framework, because every planning decision you make in Table 4 flows from your SLT calculations.

What Is Student Learning Time?

Student Learning Time (SLT) is the total number of hours required for a student to achieve the intended learning outcomes of a course. It includes all learning activities undertaken by students — whether those activities are facilitated by academic staff, conducted online, completed independently, or undertaken through physical face-to-face sessions.

SLT is not the same as contact hours. Contact hours capture only the time students spend in direct interaction with academic staff. SLT captures the complete learning effort: watching videos, reading resources, participating in discussions, completing quizzes, preparing assignments, revising for assessments, and any physical sessions. It is the broadest measure of student workload and the foundation for credit allocation.

At Sunway University, the credit-to-hours conversion is:
1 Credit = 40 SLT Hours
In accordance with the Malaysian Qualifications Framework (MQF)
A 3-credit course = 120 SLT hours • A 4-credit course = 160 SLT hours

This means that when you design a course, you work backwards from the credit value. A 3-credit course requires 120 hours of total student learning time. Your job is to distribute those 120 hours across appropriate learning activities — and to ensure that at least 80% of them qualify as ODL delivery.

Components of SLT

The total SLT of a course is assembled from several categories of learning activity. Each category contributes to the total SLT, but not all categories contribute equally to the ODL delivery requirement. Here are the components you will work with:

💻

Synchronous Online Learning

Live online sessions: lectures, tutorials, webinars, virtual labs, consultations. Counts as contact hours, ODL delivery, and SLT.

📖

Asynchronous Online Learning

Structured self-paced activities: recorded lectures, digital modules, discussion forums, multimedia resources. Counts as ODL delivery and SLT (not contact hours).

📝

Independent Learning

Self-directed activities: reading, assignment preparation, research, revision. Always counts as SLT. May count as ODL when integrated into the ODL design.

🏫

Physical Face-to-Face Sessions

On-campus activities: labs, clinical training, studio sessions, fieldwork. Counts as contact hours and SLT. Does NOT count as ODL delivery.

Assessment activities also count. Time spent on quizzes, tests, assignments, e-portfolios, presentations, and examination preparation is part of total SLT. Make sure you allocate hours for assessment in your SLT calculations — a common oversight is to account only for teaching and learning activities while forgetting the time students spend being assessed.

What Counts as ODL SLT?

Not every hour of SLT qualifies as ODL delivery. This distinction is critical because the classification of a course as an ODL course depends on the proportion of SLT delivered through ODL approaches — not on the number of online activities offered.

Learning Activity Counts as SLT? Counts as ODL? Counts as Contact Hours?
Synchronous online learning Yes Yes Yes
Asynchronous online learning Yes Yes No
Independent learning (structured, ODL-integrated) Yes Conditional No
Independent learning (not ODL-integrated) Yes No No
Physical face-to-face sessions Yes No Yes
Assessment activities (online) Yes Yes No
Assessment activities (physical) Yes No No

Notice the conditional entry for independent learning. Independent learning counts as ODL delivery only when it is intentionally designed and integrated into the ODL learning environment — for example, when students complete guided reading tasks linked to LMS resources, or prepare assignments using online materials and discussion forums. Unstructured independent learning that is unrelated to the planned ODL design does not count towards the 80% threshold.

Do not inflate ODL hours. All ODL SLT claimed must be supported by appropriate learning design, Self-Instructional Materials (SIMs), digital learning resources, online activities, assessments, and evidence of student engagement. You cannot simply label hours as "independent learning" and count them all as ODL without documentation.

In this example, 10 of the 40 independent learning hours are not integrated into the ODL design (perhaps unstructured reading without LMS guidance). Those 10 hours still count as SLT, but they do not contribute to the ODL percentage. The result is 83.3% ODL delivery, which meets the 80% threshold.

The 80% is calculated against total SLT, not against ODL activities only. The denominator is always the full 120 hours (or whatever the total SLT is for your course). The numerator includes only the hours that qualify as ODL delivery: synchronous online, asynchronous online, ODL-integrated independent learning, and online assessments.

Before looking at a common mistake, let's see what correct SLT planning looks like in practice.

Good Practice in Action: Dr. Aisyah's ODL Course

Dr. Aisyah is designing a 4-credit ODL course in Educational Technology (160 SLT hours). She begins by listing every learning activity her students will undertake, then carefully classifies each one according to whether it counts as ODL delivery.

Learning Activity Hours ODL? Dr. Aisyah's Rationale
Synchronous online (live lectures, webinars, group consultations) 30 Yes Real-time interaction via video conferencing
Asynchronous online (recorded mini-lectures, discussion forums, digital modules) 40 Yes Structured, CLO-aligned activities on the LMS
Independent learning — guided reading with LMS reflection prompts 35 Yes Linked to LMS resources; students submit reflections online
Independent learning — personal revision and exam preparation 15 No Self-directed; not structured through the LMS
Assessment (online quizzes, e-portfolio, peer review) 20 Yes All assessments submitted and graded via the LMS
Physical F2F (one on-campus orientation workshop) 20 No Face-to-face; pedagogically justified for team-building
Total ODL = 30 + 40 + 35 + 20 125
Total SLT 160
ODL % = 125 ÷ 160 78.1% Below 80%

Dr. Aisyah notices the 78.1% falls just short. Rather than inflating her independent learning hours, she takes a transparent approach: she converts 5 hours of the unstructured revision time into a guided online revision module on the LMS (with practice quizzes and discussion threads). This shifts those 5 hours from "non-ODL independent" to "ODL-integrated independent," bringing her ODL total to 130 out of 160 = 81.3%.

Why this works: Dr. Aisyah correctly separates her independent learning into two rows — the LMS-integrated portion and the unstructured portion — so her ODL calculation is transparent and auditable. She also documents a rationale for every classification decision. When the initial calculation fell short, she addressed the gap by improving her learning design (adding a guided revision module) rather than by re-labelling hours. Her physical F2F component (20 hours = 12.5% of SLT) is within the 20% cap and has a clear pedagogical justification. This is exactly the kind of documentation that MQA assessors look for in Table 4.

Now let's look at what happens when these principles are not followed.

Case: Dr. Kumar's Credit Confusion

Dr. Kumar is designing a 4-credit ODL course (160 SLT hours). He allocates 40 hours of synchronous online sessions, 50 hours of asynchronous online activities, 50 hours of independent learning, and 20 hours of physical face-to-face lab sessions. He calculates his ODL delivery as follows: "40 + 50 + 50 = 140 hours of ODL out of 160 total. That's 87.5%, so the course qualifies." He submits his Table 4 with confidence.

What's wrong here? Dr. Kumar has counted all 50 hours of independent learning as ODL delivery without checking whether those hours are integrated into the ODL design. If only 30 of the 50 independent learning hours are structured and ODL-integrated (linked to LMS resources, guided activities, discussion forums), the actual ODL calculation is: 40 (sync) + 50 (async) + 30 (structured independent) = 120 hours of ODL out of 160 total SLT = 75%. That falls below the 80% threshold. Additionally, the 20 hours of physical face-to-face sessions (12.5% of SLT) are within the 20% cap but do not count as ODL. Dr. Kumar needs to either increase his ODL-integrated independent learning hours, add more asynchronous activities, or reduce the non-ODL portion to reach 80%.

Summary

Student Learning Time is the total time required for a student to achieve a course's learning outcomes. At Sunway, 1 credit equals 40 SLT hours. All learning activities — synchronous, asynchronous, independent, physical, and assessment — contribute to total SLT. However, only activities delivered through online and distance approaches (synchronous online, asynchronous online, structured and ODL-integrated independent learning, and online assessments) count towards the 80% ODL delivery threshold.
