Independent Learning in ODL

The delivery method with the most nuance — when it counts towards ODL and when it doesn't

You have now covered the two online delivery methods: synchronous (real-time, instructor-led) and asynchronous (self-paced, staff-designed). Both count as ODL delivery. The third method — independent learning — is different. It is student-directed rather than staff-led, and whether it counts towards your ODL delivery percentage depends on how it is designed and integrated. This page explains the conditions under which independent learning qualifies, and the common mistakes that disqualify it.

What Is Independent Learning?

Independent learning refers to self-directed learning activities undertaken by students outside scheduled synchronous sessions. It represents the time students spend engaging with learning activities, applying knowledge, conducting research, completing assignments, reflecting on learning, and preparing for assessments in order to achieve the intended Course Learning Outcomes.

Independent learning is a fundamental component of higher education and forms part of the total SLT allocated to a course. In an ODL environment, it supports flexibility, learner autonomy, critical thinking, and lifelong learning skills.

How it differs from asynchronous learning: Asynchronous learning is designed and facilitated by the lecturer — the lecturer creates the materials and structures the activities. Independent learning is directed by the student, but within a framework of guidance, resources, and expectations established by the lecturer. The student drives the activity; the lecturer sets the boundaries.

Characteristics

Student-Directed

Students take responsibility for managing their own learning time, choosing resources, and completing activities.

Lecturer-Guided

Supported by appropriate guidance, learning resources, activities, and assessment requirements established by the lecturer.

Outside Scheduled Sessions

Takes place independently of synchronous or structured asynchronous sessions — the student decides when and how.

CLO-Aligned

Activities contribute to achieving specific Course Learning Outcomes and are connected to assessment requirements.

What Independent Learning Counts As

This is where independent learning becomes the most nuanced delivery method. Unlike synchronous and asynchronous learning, which always count as ODL delivery, independent learning sometimes counts.

Independent Learning Counts As:

Student Learning Time ODL Delivery (conditional) Contact Hours

Independent learning always counts as Student Learning Time — students are spending time on learning activities, and that time is legitimate regardless of the delivery mode.

Independent learning never counts as contact hours — there is no direct interaction between academic staff and students.

But whether independent learning counts towards the 80% ODL delivery threshold depends on one critical condition:

Does Your Independent Learning Count Towards ODL Delivery?

  • ?
    Is the independent learning activity intentionally designed and integrated into the ODL learning environment (e.g., linked from the LMS, aligned with specific CLOs, supported by guidance and resources)?
  • ✓
    If yes: The activity counts as ODL delivery. It contributes towards the 80% threshold.
  • ×
    If no: The activity counts as SLT only. It does not contribute to the 80% ODL threshold.
Do not inflate your numbers. Independent learning hours must not be used to artificially inflate ODL delivery percentages. Programmes must be able to demonstrate a clear relationship between the independent learning activities, Course Learning Outcomes, learning resources, assessment requirements, and expected student learning outcomes.

Applying the Independent Learning Rule

You now understand the conditional nature of independent learning in ODL. The critical test is whether the activity is intentionally designed and integrated into the ODL learning environment. The two cases below illustrate the difference between independent learning that qualifies and independent learning that does not.

Case: Qualifying vs. Non-Qualifying Independent Learning

Course A (qualifying): Dr. Nadia allocates 20 hours of independent learning for her ODL course. On the LMS, she provides a curated reading list linked to specific CLOs, a structured assignment brief with marking rubric, weekly reflection prompts, and a discussion forum where students post their progress. The activities are clearly connected to assessments and CLOs.

Why it qualifies: The independent learning is intentionally designed, integrated into the LMS, supported by guidance and resources, and has a clear relationship to CLOs and assessment. The 20 hours may count towards ODL delivery.

Course B (non-qualifying): Dr. Hafiz allocates 25 hours of independent learning. His instructions read: "Spend time researching topics related to this week's lecture." No reading list is provided, no structured activity is described, no connection to CLOs or assessments is stated, and there is no mechanism for tracking or guiding the activity.

Why it does not qualify: The independent learning is vague, unstructured, and disconnected from the ODL learning design. There is no evidence of intentional integration. These 25 hours count as SLT only — they do not contribute to the 80% ODL threshold.

Summary

Independent learning is the most conditional delivery method. It always counts as SLT, never counts as contact hours, and counts towards ODL delivery only when intentionally designed and integrated into the ODL learning environment. The key test is whether there is a clear, demonstrable relationship between the activity, the CLOs, the learning resources, and the assessment requirements.
