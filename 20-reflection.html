Key Takeaways

As you complete this course, here are the core principles that should guide your ODL practice. These eight messages have been reinforced throughout all 20 pages. Keep them in mind as you develop, deliver, and refine your ODL courses.

ODL Means ≥80% Online SLT

A course qualifies as ODL only when at least 80% of total Student Learning Time is delivered through online approaches (synchronous online, asynchronous, and LMS-independent learning). This is the regulatory threshold, not a suggestion.

SLT Is Not Duration

Student Learning Time measures the actual time students spend learning, not the scheduled duration of an activity. For asynchronous and independent learning, always multiply estimated engagement time by 2–3× to account for pausing, re-reading, and reflection.

Table 4 Is Your Blueprint

Table 4 is the central document of your ODL course design. Everything flows from it — your CLOs, activities, assessments, and SLT distribution must all be documented and aligned within this single template. A strong Table 4 makes everything else easier.

LMS Is a Learning Environment

The Learning Management System is not a file repository. It is a structured learning environment where students engage with content, interact with peers and instructors, complete activities, and receive feedback. Design it as a learning experience, not a document library.

Constructive Alignment

Every element of your course must connect: CLO → Learning Activity → Assessment → SLT. The verb in each CLO tells you the cognitive level required. Activities prepare students for that level. Assessments measure achievement at that level. SLT allocates time for that level.

RSI Must Be Regular AND Substantive

Regular and Substantive Interaction is a requirement, not a recommendation. “Regular” means scheduled and consistent. “Substantive” means focused on course content and learning — not just administrative announcements. Plan RSI before the semester starts.

Monitor Continuously

Do not wait until the semester ends to check whether students are engaging. Use LMS analytics to monitor login frequency, content access, assessment submission, and participation on a weekly basis. Define intervention triggers and act on them early.

Risk Checklist as Quality Gate

The common risks checklist from Page 18 is your final quality gate. Run through it before submitting your course for approval and again before each semester begins. A plan that passes every item on that list has a strong foundation for quality ODL delivery.

These principles are interconnected. You cannot separate SLT calculation from Table 4 design, or constructive alignment from assessment planning, or RSI from student support. ODL Programme Development is a system — each component reinforces the others. When one element is weak, it weakens the entire course. When every element is strong, the result is a coherent, quality learning experience that meets regulatory requirements and serves students well.

You will learn the six attributes of effective SIMs, practice writing for self-directed learners, and develop actual SIM content for your course. If ODL Programme Development taught you to plan the blueprint, Effective SIM Writing teaches you to build the house.
